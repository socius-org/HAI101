What can machines teach us about minds?

That is the question behind HAI101: Human x Artificial Intelligence, a lecture series I'm
organising at LSE this autumn with the Centre for Philosophy of Natural and Social Science.

Machines now imitate people well enough that researchers use them as proxies: to run an
experiment before recruiting a participant, to predict how a group will behave, to test a
theory at a scale no human study could reach. At the same time, the machines have become
something to study in their own right. The questions we once asked only of people are being
asked of them.

Thirteen speakers from Stanford, MIT, Penn, UCL, Utrecht, Oxford, Groningen, Princeton, Yale
and LSE, over four Fridays:

• 23 Oct · [Steven Feng], [Nick Oh], [Fernand Gobet], [Youngjin Yoo]
• 6 Nov · [Helen Jin], [Megan Peters], [Maximilian Noichl], [Nicholas Bishop], [Matthia Sabatelli]
• 13 Nov · [Jessica Thompson], [Akshay Jagadish], [Gabrielle Kaili-May Liu]
• 27 Nov · Industry Day, with [Benjamin Manning] first to confirm

Free and in person at LSE. Each Friday has its own ticket, and if you have never met a neural
network, we wrote a note to read beforehand: Introduction to Transformers and LLMs, with
[Steven Feng].

Programme, abstracts, reading and registration: socialscience.ai

OPTION B · THE IDEA FIRST
=====================================================================

The people who founded AI were not computer scientists. Herbert Simon was a political scientist
with a Nobel in economics and psychology's top award. The perceptron was built by a psychologist.
The newest "reasoning" models are described in the words of Daniel Kahneman.

The disciplines that study humans have always been inside AI. HAI101 puts them back in the room.

Human x Artificial Intelligence is a lecture series at LSE, hosted by the Centre for Philosophy
of Natural and Social Science, asking what machines can teach us about minds, and what minds can
teach the machines. Thirteen speakers over four Fridays:

23 Oct · machines as learners and instruments — [Steven Feng], [Nick Oh], [Fernand Gobet], [Youngjin Yoo]
6 Nov · machines examined as minds — [Helen Jin], [Megan Peters], [Maximilian Noichl], [Nicholas Bishop], [Matthia Sabatelli]
13 Nov · what this does to science — [Jessica Thompson], [Akshay Jagadish], [Gabrielle Kaili-May Liu]
27 Nov · Industry Day, the same questions asked by builders — [Benjamin Manning] and more

Free and in person. Each Friday has its own ticket. If you have never met a neural network, we
wrote the primer: Introduction to Transformers and LLMs.

OPTION C · FOR THE COLLABORATIVE POST (speakers as co-authors)
=====================================================================

We are speaking at HAI101: Human x Artificial Intelligence, a lecture series at LSE this autumn.

Between us we work on language models that learn like children, neural networks as stand-ins
for human participants, what chess tells us about expertise in the age of AI, value creation at
runtime, measuring understanding and testing for consciousness in machines, how models travel
between sciences, bounded rationality at collective scale, cognitive-like learning in networks,
behavioural clones as scientific instruments, whether LLMs are the beginning or end of cognitive
science, and reliability through metacognition. One question runs through all of it: what can
machines teach us about minds, and what can minds teach the machines?

Four Fridays at LSE, hosted by the Centre for Philosophy of Natural and Social Science:

• 23 Oct · [Steven Feng], [Nick Oh], [Fernand Gobet], [Youngjin Yoo]
