DESCRIPTION
=====================================================================
Opening lecture of HAI101: Human x Artificial Intelligence, a lecture series at the London School of Economics on what machines can teach us about minds, and what the study of minds can teach the machines. Recorded on Friday 23 October 2026 in The LSE Lecture Theatre.

In fifteen minutes, one idea and one map. The idea: the people who founded AI were not computer scientists. In the decade from 1943 to 1956, a neurophysiologist and a logician built the first artificial neuron, an economist founded game theory and designed the computer, a political scientist showed that people satisfice rather than optimise, a psychologist proposed the learning rule, and a mathematician published the imitation game in a philosophy journal. AI and cognitive science were born in the same summer. The lecture follows that braid of five strands (social science, behavioural science, cognitive science, philosophy and AI) from Herbert Simon's EPAM and Chase and Simon's chess experiments, through Kahneman's System 1 and System 2 to today's reasoning models, and through two LSE economists: Friedrich Hayek, whose 1952 theory of mind is cited on page 388 of Rosenblatt's perceptron paper, and Charles Goodhart, whose law names the way reward models go wrong.

The map: the four questions the series asks of its fourteen speakers. Machines as instruments, as learners, examined as minds, and as disruptors.

Series programme, abstracts and bios: https://socialscience.ai
